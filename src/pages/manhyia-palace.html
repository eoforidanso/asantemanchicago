Manhyia · Kumasi, Ghana

Manhyia, the seat of the Asantehene.

The palace in Kumasi that is the seat and official residence of the Asantehene.

A gathering of the people

Manhyia comes from the Twi Oman hyia, “the gathering of the people.” It is the seat of the Asantehene and his official residence in Kumasi. Today that is Otumfuo Osei Tutu II, the 16th Asantehene, who is also the spiritual, cultural and political head of Asanteman.

The Chicago association’s own court follows the same traditional divisions: Akwamu, Adonten, Nifa, Benkum, Kyidom and Gyaase. Meet the people who hold them in Chicago.

Two palaces

There are two palaces at Manhyia. The older one was built by the British in 1925 as a residence for Asantehene Agyeman Prempeh I, who had returned from exile. Later, Otumfuo Opoku Ware II built a new palace close by, and that is the one the Asantehene lives in today.

The 1925 building was opened as the Manhyia Palace Museum in 1995 by Otumfuo Opoku Ware II.

Accounts differ on some early details of the palace’s history, so this page keeps to what published sources agree on. If AACM holds a fuller account, we would be glad to use it.
